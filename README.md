## About the research

This repository documents the development of a doctoral research project in
Philosophy centered on the concept of **Sovereignty of Hospitality**
(*Soberanía de la Hospitalidad*).

The research examines a fundamental problem of contemporary political
philosophy: modern sovereignty has historically been constructed around a
privileged conception of the human subject — autonomous, rational, sovereign,
capable of language, decision and political representation — while other forms
of existence have been positioned outside or below the political community.

This structure of exclusion is currently challenged from two directions.

On one side, contemporary debates on **animality** undermine the supposedly
stable boundary separating the human from the non-human animal.

On the other, the development of **artificial intelligence and algorithmic
governance** challenges several capacities traditionally invoked to justify
human exceptionalism, including language, calculation, decision-making,
prediction and increasingly complex forms of artificial agency.

The dissertation investigates what happens to sovereignty when the political
identity of the human can no longer be securely founded upon these exclusions.

Rather than responding to this crisis by expanding domination or transferring
sovereign authority to computational systems, the project proposes the concept
of **Sovereignty of Hospitality**: a political philosophy in which sovereignty
is reconstructed through responsibility, interdependence, vulnerability,
cohabitation and the internal limitation of power.

---

## Central research problem

Modern political sovereignty has frequently operated through mechanisms of
appropriation, exclusion and control over forms of life and material
environments considered external to the sovereign subject.

The human being becomes politically sovereign by defining what it is not:
animal, object, nature, machine, foreigner or other.

The dissertation asks whether political sovereignty can be conceived without
requiring this logic of absolute appropriation and exclusion.

The contemporary emergence of artificial intelligence intensifies this
problem. While computational systems increasingly participate in decisions
affecting territories, economies, institutions and living beings, political
responsibility may become dispersed among algorithms, infrastructures,
corporations and states.

The resulting problem is therefore double:

> How can political sovereignty be rethought once both animality and artificial
> intelligence destabilize the boundaries through which modern political
> thought defined the human sovereign subject?

---

## Research question

The central question of the dissertation is:

> **If political identity is constituted through its relation with alterity,**
> **can sovereignty be conceived in such a way that its own constitution depends**
> **not upon exclusion and domination of the other, but upon openness,**
> **responsibility and hospitality toward alterity?**

This question is developed through three interconnected fields:

- human / animal difference;
- sovereignty / alterity / hospitality;
- human decision / artificial intelligence / algorithmic governance.

---

## Central hypothesis

The dissertation advances the hypothesis that the contemporary crisis of human
exceptionalism requires more than an extension of classical sovereignty toward
new subjects.

It requires a transformation of the concept of sovereignty itself.

The project proposes **Sovereignty of Hospitality** as a form of political
sovereignty whose legitimacy does not arise from unrestricted control over
life, territory or alterity, but from its capacity to assume responsibility for
the relations of interdependence that make political existence possible.

Under this model, sovereignty becomes internally limited by the existence of
others whose vulnerability, difference and interests cannot legitimately be
reduced to objects of sovereign disposal.

The political question therefore changes from:

> *What are we capable of doing with the other?*

to:

> **With what right may we dispose of the other?**

## Original contribution

The principal theoretical contribution of the dissertation is the formulation
and systematic development of the concept of **Sovereignty of Hospitality**.

The concept seeks to move beyond the traditional opposition between:

- absolute sovereignty;
- and the simple abolition of sovereignty.

Instead, the project explores a sovereignty capable of limiting itself through
the recognition of constitutive interdependence.

The proposed model is organized around eight principles:

1. **Interdependence**
2. **Vulnerability**
3. **Non-absolute appropriation**
4. **Accountable responsibility**
5. **Irreducible plurality**
6. **Shared territoriality**
7. **Representation of absent interests**
8. **Internal limitation of political power**

These principles provide the normative basis for investigating what a
political community beyond strict anthropocentrism could mean.

## From interspecies community to more-than-human political community

The project initially emerged from the philosophical problem of the relation
between sovereignty and animality.

Its development progressively expands the problem toward a
**more-than-human political community**.

The dissertation therefore studies political relations among:

- human beings;
- non-human animals;
- ecosystems and territories;
- technological infrastructures;
- artificial intelligence and algorithmic systems.

This does not imply attributing identical political status to humans, animals,
ecosystems and machines.

The research instead asks how political institutions should account for forms
of alterity and interdependence that participate in, sustain or are affected by
the political community without necessarily possessing the classical
characteristics of the sovereign human subject.

## Philosophical framework

The research develops primarily through a critical dialogue with:

### Jacques Derrida

Derrida provides the central philosophical axis of the dissertation through
his work on:

- hospitality;
- sovereignty;
- animality;
- alterity;
- deconstruction;
- the limits of the human;
- responsibility;
- and the relation between unconditional demands and political institutions.

His analyses of the animal, hospitality and sovereignty provide the conceptual
terrain from which the notion of **Sovereignty of Hospitality** is developed.

### Friedrich Nietzsche

Nietzsche contributes to the genealogical critique of the categories through
which Western philosophy has constructed humanity, morality, domination and
life.

### Martin Heidegger

Heidegger is examined particularly in relation to world, dwelling, technology
and the philosophical distinction between human existence and animality.

### Emmanuel Lévinas

Lévinas contributes the ethical problem of responsibility before alterity and
the possibility that responsibility precedes sovereign autonomy.

### Giorgio Agamben

Agamben provides an important framework for analyzing sovereignty,
anthropological division, bare life and the mechanisms through which political
orders define their internal and external limits.

### Kate Crawford and contemporary critical AI studies

Contemporary analyses of artificial intelligence are used to investigate the
material, territorial, political and ecological infrastructures underlying
computational systems and algorithmic governance.

## Research objectives

### General objective

To formulate the philosophical concept of **Sovereignty of Hospitality** and
develop the normative foundations of a more-than-human political community
through a critique of anthropocentric sovereignty, animal exclusion and
emerging forms of computational sovereignty.

### Specific objectives

The research seeks to:

1. reconstruct the philosophical genealogy of the human/animal distinction;
2. analyze how human exceptionalism participates in the constitution of modern
   sovereignty;
3. examine the philosophical implications of animality for the limits of the
   political subject;
4. analyze how artificial intelligence destabilizes traditional criteria of
   human exceptionalism;
5. investigate algorithmic governance and the redistribution of political
   decision-making through computational systems;
6. develop hospitality as a political category rather than exclusively an
   ethical relation;
7. formulate the concept and principles of **Sovereignty of Hospitality**;
8. explore the institutional implications of a more-than-human political
   community;
9. develop the conceptual model of the **Hospitable City** as a possible field
   of institutional application.

## Provisional dissertation architecture

The research is currently structured around three major movements.

### Part I — The limits of the human

Investigation of the philosophical construction of humanity through its
distinction from animality, nature and other forms of alterity.

### Part II — The crisis of sovereignty

Analysis of sovereignty, human exceptionalism, artificial intelligence and the
emergence of algorithmic forms of political decision.

### Part III — Sovereignty of Hospitality

Development of hospitality as a political category and systematic formulation
of the original concept of **Sovereignty of Hospitality**, followed by its
implications for more-than-human political community.

The eighth chapter is projected as the conceptual center of the dissertation,
where the concept of Sovereignty of Hospitality is formally developed.

## What this repository is

This repository is not the dissertation itself.

It is the **research infrastructure through which the dissertation is being
developed**.

It records the evolution of:

source → reading → note → interpretation → argument → objection → revision →
chapter

The repository is designed to make the intellectual development of the
research auditable while maintaining a clear distinction between:

- primary sources;
- secondary literature;
- researcher notes;
- verified quotations;
- AI-assisted analysis;
- authorial arguments;
- dissertation drafts.

## Artificial intelligence and human authorship

Artificial intelligence is used in this project as a research instrument.

It may assist with tasks such as:

- bibliographic exploration;
- document organization;
- comparison of arguments;
- identification of conceptual relations;
- transcription and OCR workflows;
- linguistic revision;
- testing objections;
- and research infrastructure development.

AI output is **not treated as philosophical evidence or academic authority**.

No AI-generated reference, quotation, attribution or interpretation is accepted
as verified merely because a model produced it.

The intellectual responsibility for the thesis — including its arguments,
interpretations, conclusions and final text — remains with the researcher.

The repository therefore preserves records that distinguish AI assistance from
human validation and authorial decision.

## Repository architecture

The flow of knowledge moves from questions and sources toward analysis,
writing, and review:

```text
00-gobernanza/    norms, decisions, audits, and AI use
01-investigacion/ questions, concepts, and methodology
02-fuentes/       bibliography and verifiable source records
03-analisis/      argument maps and cases
04-escritura/     outline, chapters, and appendices
05-revision/      feedback and quality control
assets/           figures and other manuscript resources
plantillas/       reusable templates
scripts/          automated checks
library/          local doctoral library and unversioned working materials
tools/library/    tools and generated outputs for processing the library
```

Each documentary directory contains a `README.md` defining its scope. The
layers, their dependencies, and traceability conventions are explained in
[`00-gobernanza/arquitectura.md`](00-gobernanza/arquitectura.md). The local
library complements these layers: it preserves consultation documents and
operational derivatives, while `02-fuentes/` contains the metadata and source
records that form part of the versioned academic record.

## Doctoral library and OCR workflow

When `library/` is used, each work may have its own space for the original in
`source/`, OCR results in `ocr/`, and transcriptions in `transcription/` or
`transcriptions/`. These materials are local inputs, not validated evidence
merely because they have been processed. Automated tool outputs are stored in
`tools/library/output/`.

Originals, copyrighted texts, private files, and OCR or transcription
derivatives are not versioned. The `.gitignore` rules protect these paths, but
they do not replace checking rights, confidentiality, and permissions before
adding any material. The recommended workflow is:

1. Preserve the authorized original in the work's local `source/` directory.
2. Run OCR or transcription without modifying the original and retain the
   output in the corresponding private paths.
3. Review the result against the source document; record errors, pagination,
   and relevant correction decisions.
4. Record metadata in `02-fuentes/bibliografia.bib` and create a verifiable
   source record in `02-fuentes/fichas/`, without copying restricted material.
5. Cite and analyze the verified source, not an unreviewed automated output.

## Methodology and traceability

Research questions guide source selection; source records distinguish
bibliographic data, reading notes, and verified quotations; and arguments link
questions and sources through stable identifiers. Drafts integrate those
arguments, while the review layer documents observations and responses.
Uncertainties, limitations, and disagreements are recorded rather than resolved
through unverified inference.

The methodological design is maintained in
[`01-investigacion/metodologia.md`](01-investigacion/metodologia.md), and the
full traceability conventions are described in the architecture document. The
presence of a source in the library, a transcription, or a summary does not by
itself establish that it has been read, is relevant, or is valid.

## AI-assisted research workflow

AI may facilitate exploratory searches, classification, comparison, summary,
style review, and automation. Every material contribution must be recorded and
checked against reliable sources. Restricted texts, personal data, and
confidential material must not be uploaded to external services without
authorization.

Philosophical interpretation, evidence selection, argument evaluation, final
writing, and conclusions remain the researcher's responsibility. Consult the
[`AI policy`](00-gobernanza/politica-ia.md) and document relevant uses with
`plantillas/registro-ia.md`.
